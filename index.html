About Me

I am originally from Pune, India. I completed my undergraduate degree in Mechanical Engineering before transitioning into Physics and Quantum Computing through self-study, research internships, and competitive projects. Before beginning my PhD, I worked on autonomous robotic surgery systems focused on kidney stone treatment and later conducted research in quantum optimization and variational quantum algorithms.

My research is about making quantum algorithms practical by reducing the shots, queries, and circuit resources they need. At UW-Madison I work on Bayesian measurement schemes for imaginary-time evolution and on fault-tolerant resource estimation. In summer 2026 I developed adaptive shot allocation for gate set tomography at Argonne National Laboratory. Broadly, I am interested in quantum algorithm design, Hamiltonian simulation, and practical pathways toward useful fault-tolerant quantum computing.

√X
Summer 2026 · Argonne National Laboratory

Adaptive Gate Set Tomography

As a Givens Associate with Dr. Matt Menickelly and Dr. Jeffrey Larson, I built an adaptive shot-allocation method for gate set tomography. Instead of measuring every circuit equally, it sends shots where they are most informative. I was also selected for the FASTMath Student Cohort, a DOE SciDAC Institute group, on my mentor's recommendation.

2.1× (1 qubit) and 2.7× (2 qubits) fewer shots

The X(π/2) gate, one of the gates that gate set tomography characterizes.
